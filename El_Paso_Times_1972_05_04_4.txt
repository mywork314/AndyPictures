ON INDIAN DANCING
At a recent political rally in Ysleta, in

front of the Old Mission, some of the spec-
tators were disappointed when the Tig-
uas did not dance. It had been announced
they would do so.

Alton Griffin, commissioner for the
Tigua Nation, called to explain why the
Indians did not dance.

“There were two reasons.” he said.
“First, the rally was held in front of the
church and the area from the front door,
out about 100 feet, is an old Indian burial
ground. The Indians will not dance in
that area.

“Second, while the Indians have
danced for President Johnson and do
dance on days that honor their patron
saints, they always vote on whether to
dance or not. They had not voted on this
and, for the first reason, would probably
have turned down the invitation.”"

The Indians are Catholics and regard
the church as “their’’ church and the
space in front of the church as the “plaza
of their pueblo.’’ I think all of us should
reszect them for their ideas.

Incidentally, a dance team is being
formed of Younger Tiguas, who will be
available for local events. Tney will dance
the ‘‘folk’’ dances, not the traditional
dances performed on saints days or for
visiting dignitaries.

Alton, it is impossible to find a tele-
phone number for the tribe under “‘Tigua”
or “Indian’’ and few people know to look
under “Texas State Government, Com-
mission fer Indian Affairs.’ Why don’t
you make it easy for the Tiguas to get

ANNIVERSARY GIFT

Have you ever thought about a proper
anniversary gift for your wife when it’s
your 26th or 3lst or 38th wedding anni-
versary?

Jack Resen, promotional director of
Bassett Center, and his wife, Mary Ellen,
have come up with something new.

Let Jack explain:

“Mary Ellen and I decided to give
each other a tree for our anniversary
(27th) and to have it planted somewhere
in the City, perhaps the Chamizal area.
We contacted Andy Cohen, president of
Beautify El Paso Association and Mrs.
‘M. H. Welch, former president, because
we wanted to work through that organi-
zation. We intend to do this every year
from now, on and just wish we had start-
ed many years ago. We feel that, in this
way, we can do something for each other
and also for El Paso.”

I think it’s a splendid idea and hope
others adopt it.

EVERYBODY’S BUSY

The wealthy tourist lost his pedigreed
dog while stopping in a small town. He
inserted an ad in the weekly paper offer-
ing a $100 reward.

The next day, he called at the newspa-
per office to inquire, but found noone
there but a decrepit janitor.

“Where’s the newspaper force?’’ he
asked impatiently,

“They're all out,” the old man
replied, ‘trying to find that dog of
yours.”’

NEWSPAPERMEN IN HEAVEN?

I hadn't given any thought to whether
or not there would be newspaper men in
heaven until Phil Holmsberg, 808 Cincin-
nati, sent me a copy of the News Letter
of St. Paul’s Lutheran Church of El Paso,
Included was this article, marked by
Phil:

Men of what profession are sure to be
in heaven? Newspapermen, that’s who,
said Charles C. Clayton, Southern Illinois
University professor of journalism.

“They won't need doctors in heaven,”
said Professor Clayton. “Nobody will
ever be sick there. They won't need
preachers. Everybody there will have
been saved. But people in the south end
of heaven will want to know what people
in the north end are doing. They'll need
newspapermen.””

Phil added this note:

“They will report it as it is, not as
they would like it to be.”
