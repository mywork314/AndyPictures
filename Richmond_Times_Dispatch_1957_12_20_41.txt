Houk’s Appointment Starts Rumors

IT WAS INEVITABLE that the rumors would start when
the Yankees brought up Ralph Houk, manager of the Denver
club, as a coach to succeeed Bill Dickey. Sure enough, there
was a wire service story about how Houk had been tapped as
Stengel’s heir.

Maybe so, but chances are Stengel will continue as Yankee
manager just as long as he continues to win those American
League pennants. His present contract has another season to
run. Moreover, there are not many, if any, mindreaders among
the New York bascball writers—and it would take a real good
mindreader to know what goes on in Yankee general manager
George Weiss’ always busy brain.

Andy Cohen, who succeeds Houk as Denver manager, is the
fellow who in 1928 succeeded the great Rogers Hornsby at sec-
ond base for John McGraw's New York Giants. Cohen has had
a long and successful managerial career in the minors. During
the 1942 baseball season, Cohen was stationed at the Richmond
Air Base, and made many friends here.
