Miller Lowlife’

WESTERN ATHLETIC CONFERENCE football
players named Miller this week were almost as unlucky
as the man who steered the Titanic into an iceberg. Willie
Miller, Colorado State’s gifted wide receiver, recived a
hypertension of the elbow which may keep him out of the
Rams’ first game that will be played next Saturday
against New Mexico. Jay Miller, Brigham Young’s super
Pass catcher, got an even worse injury — a broken
shoulder blade. The medical term is “‘transverse frac-
ture of the right scapula.’’ He’s likely to miss the first
three BYU games. Joe Watts of the Provo, Utah, Herald,
may have made newspaper history by running pictures
of Miller’s X-rays. Other Millers in the news this week,
Dick and Mark, were more fortunate. Dick, former Sun
Kings’ business manager and radio broadcaster.
has returned to El Paso from Scottsdale, Ariz., where he
managed a radio station. His son Mark, who played
second base for the UTEP Miners last season, has signed
a contract with the Texas Rangers and probably will be
assigned to Gastonia in the Eastern Carolina League
next spring. Andy Cohen of UTEP has lost a good in-
fielder. Golfer after an 18-hole match: “I lost four balls
and three friends.’’ A popular name for UTEP’s football
team in the pass-minded days of Bobby Dobbs was
“Flying Miners.’’ John Mooney in the 1974 NCAA
Football Guide calls them the “Trying Miners.”’ Football
teams, one man Says, have always had the triple option:
Win, lose or draw.
