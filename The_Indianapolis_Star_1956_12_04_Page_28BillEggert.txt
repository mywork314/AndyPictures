BASEBALL FANS won't meet Indianapolis’ new mana-
ger, Andy Cohen, until some time in February, but here's
a characteristic incident offered as an introduction.

Twenty years ago Cohen was second-basing for the
Minneapolis Millers when the Millers, managed by Ownie
Bush, trampled other league teams with such triple-A favor-
ites as Joe Hauser, Buzz Arlett, Fabian Gaffke and Walt
Tauscher,

At that time Jimmy Theofanis was hawking the Indi-
anapolis Star at the Claypool Hotel corner. Cohen, a Jew,
and Theofanis, a little Greek kid, became close friends.
Cohen was Jimmy’s hero and the hero saw that Jimmy got
in free to Miller games at Perry Stadium and was intro-
duced to all the Miller players.

One night during a pouring rain, Cohen purchased all
of Jimmy's newspapers on the promise that Jimmy would
go home so he wouldn't catch a cold, Andy gave a dollar
and never took the papers.

Jimmy now lives in Rochester, N.Y., and he writes
back, “He (Cohen) was a wonderful man and I'm sure that
the years of experience behind him will make him a tre-
mendous inspirational leader for youngsters. They in turn
will respect and admire him for his eagerness to help them
and last but not least for his sincerity and loyalty.”
