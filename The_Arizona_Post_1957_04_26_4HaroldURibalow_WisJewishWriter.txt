SOME MONTHS ago, in this space, I commented
on the past career of Andy Cohen, the New York
Giant second-baseman in the 1920’s, the man who
succeeded, for a few years, the great immortal Rogers
Hornsby, and who was one of the first Jewish players

to hit the big time.
Andy is now the manager of the Indianapolis

(American Association) club.

During spring training, some of the reporters
talked with him about old times.

OF COURSE, they reviewed Andy’s career, but
of some interest, apart from the statistics, is what
Andy said about the men who managed him. “I played
under three wonderful managers during my active
career,” he stated. “McGraw, Tris Speaker and Donie
Bush. They were all great baseball men, and I profited
tremendously by their teachings.

“As far as McGraw was concerned, you either
liked him or hated him. There was no in-between. He
was a hard loser, but certainly not a poor loser. i,
personally, loved the guy.” Which is rather remark-
able, for Andy was let go by the Giants when he sport-
ed a batting average of .294—rather good at any time.
