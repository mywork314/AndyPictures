HOTLINE FEEDBACK — ‘‘To set
the record straight, in 1926 I was a
rookie up from the Texas League
(with the New York Giants) when
Travis Jackson injured his leg. I filled
in a few games that year, 19 or 20, and
batted only .259. However, in 1926
Frank Frisch was the regular second
sacker and got traded in ‘27 for
Rogers Hornsby, who was traded
later that season to the Boston Braves
for Shorty Hogan and Jimmy Welsh.

“So in 1928 I became the regular se-
cond baseman, batting .274. Hornsby
hit .387. He not only beat me but led
the entire league. The next year
(1929) I hit .294. While by today’s
standards that is considered respec-
table, I eventually was sent down to
Newark for Doc itt, a lefthanded
screwball pitcher. Later, I managed
all over the minors, inclduing Denver
and New Orleans. My last job in the
pros was in Philadelphia as a coach in
1960. I am now the baseball coach at
Texas El Paso. I might have been a
disappointment to some people, but
not to myself. I am proud of my
career as a player, manager, coach
and scout.’ — Andy Cohen, El Paso.
