Rotary
Chief Visits
Border

Approximately 560 Rotarians
from Texas and Mexico were
present in the Juarez Country
Club to hear president of
Rotary International, William
E. Walk Jr., of Ontario, Calif.,
speak during his visit to the
border Thursday.

Though scheduled to arrive
in El! Paso Thursday morning,
Walk’s flight was detained in
Chicago because of heavy fog.
He did not arrive in El Paso
until after 7 p.m. and was
immediately taken to Juarez

Wolf, representing Mayor
Peter deWetter.

Special guests at the ban-
quet included Alderman Wolf,
El Paso County Judge Udell
Moore and Juarez Mayor Ber-
nardo Norzagaray. Rotary
leaders attending he banquet
included governor of
District 552, Walter Wade, and
Mexican Dist. Gov. Carlos
Aldana, District 411.

Walk, a prominent attorney
in California, was appointed to
the judiciary in 1969 by Gov.
Ronald Reagan. He has been a
Rotarian since 1948 and has
served Rotary International as
first vice president, chairman
of the executive committee of
the Board of Directors, direc-
. tor, parliamentarian, district
governor, Rotary Foundation
trustee and committee chair-
man and member.

He has received the Brother-
hood Award of the National
Conference of Christian and

_ Jews as the Protestant repre-

sentative and the Chamber of
Commerce Award.

Walk will leave El Paso Fri-
day morning for Houston.
