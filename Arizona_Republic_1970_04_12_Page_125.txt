Arizona and Texas-E] Paso
divided a Western Athletic
Conference doubleheader, UA
winning the first game, 6-5,

The second-game loss was
a setback for coach Frank
Sancet's Wildcats (28-8) in
the WAC southern division
race. UA, 2-1 in league play,
needed a sweep of the
three-game series.

But the elements and the
inability of Sancet’s No. 2 and
No. 3 pitchers to get people
out cost the Wildcats an im-
portant victory. It almost cost
them two losses.

A violent windstorm played
havoc with everything hit into
the air—and some of the rou-
tine grounders to the infield
were even windblown,

Fourteen doubles were car-
omed around Dudley Field
and off its fences. And only a
couple were honest two-base
blows.

The pique of the moment
was captured by UTEP cen-
terfielder Brian Harper in the
top of the fourth inning of the
second game.

After a long, long chase of
a ball hit by UA’s Steve Mik-
ulic, the wind blew it just
past Harper’s glove for a dou-
ble. In frustration, Harper
threw his glove against the
fence.

In the opener, UA starter
Larry Dierks lasted through
three and a half batters. He
walked three and left with a
partial count on another —
who also walked.

But Mickey O’Hara picked
up for Dierks and went all
the way for his fifth win.

He had some shaky mo-
ments and almost lost it in
the bottom of the ninth.
UTEP scored twice and had
the bases loaded when pitch-
er Larry Smith struck out.

Normally, a pitcher isn’t
sent to the plate to bat in that
kind of situation.
