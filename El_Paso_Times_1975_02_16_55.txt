Red Raiders Whip Miners

By MARK LEIBSON
‘Times Sports Writer

If Texas Tech hadn't been so greedy in
the first game, maybe the Red Raiders
would have returned to Lubbock with a
Split of the four-game series against the
University of Texas at El Paso. ~

Instead, as Coach Kal Segrist's Raiders
drove across run after run, 14 total in the
first game Staurday, valubable daylight
started turning into darkness.

And so having captured the opener from
the Miners 14-4 at Dudley Field, Texas
Tech had also built an impressive 8-2
advantage in the nightcap only to have the
umpires call the game in the top of the fith

inning.

Since U.T. El Paso hadn't had its-turn at
“bat, the game was declared no contest and
Tech left town with a 1-2 record while
Coach Andy Cohen’s Miners lucked out
with a 2-1 mark.

“We're lucky the umps called the
game,” said Cohen. “‘A 2-1 record is a lot
better than splitting the four-game
series.””

U.T. El Paso opened the afternoon with
the form and determination which carried
the Miners to two victories Saturday over
Tech.

With Roy Stringer on the mound, the Red
Raiders struck first when Jim Horton
singled to lead off the second inning, stole
second and third and scored on Gary Ash-
by’s sacrifice fly to deep center field.

But Stringer took matters into his own
hands in the bottom of the frame when he
sent a Kim Hunter pitch high over the right
field wall, making the score read 1-1.

The Miners went ahead in the third when
Art Bravo walked with one away. Steve
Dau singled to left and Glenn Villareal
cracked a double between center and right
scoring both runners ahead of him.

One more Miner crossed the plate an
inning later, started when Keith Rushing
was nicked by Hunter’s pitch. David
Nance and Bravo followed with back-to-
back singles loading the bags.

Segrist pulled his starter in favor of
former Austin High School star David
Runkel who proceeded to walk the next
Miner batter, Dau, on four pitches to force
Rushing home.

But then the little redhead bore down
and struck out the next two Miner batters,
leaving the sacks full.

From that point on, Runkle was flawless
except for a fifth inning triple from Terry
Dillingham.

‘Texas Tech exploded in the fifth for
seven runs on five hits while sending 12
men to the plate. With two outs and a
runner on first. Stringer lost control of his
composure by walking Bryan Cowan and
giving up a run scoring double to Ronnie
Mattson.

Kim Nikkel followed with a line shot to
right which just cleared the fence and gave
Tech a 5-4 advantage. . :

“I had thoughts about intentionally
walking Nikkel,” explained Cohen, ‘‘but_
decided not to because another walk would
have forced in a run.

After Nikkel’s homer, Cohen sent Bob,

Knox to the mound to cool Tech’s tem-

perature. But the right-hander had his
Problems by allowing a walk and three
singles with a hit batter sandwiched in
between for three additional tallies.
Reliever Mike Hilton ended the rally by
forcing Bob Wiebe to pop up to the infield.

“It’s funny how fast things can change,”
chuckled Cohen. “Yesterday, our pitching
was real good but today, we didn’t have a
‘stopper.””

With the outcome already determined,
Texas Tech continued its scoring spree
with six more runs in the sixth.

Game No. 2 was started over an hour
late and by the third frame, the dusty skies
of El Paso drew increasingly darker. With
Tech at bat with two outs in the fifth,
leading 7-3, the umpires decided play

id be stopped.

plain, it was getting dark out there.”

Segrist was pleased though with the way
his team broke out of its two-game batting

slump. “I've known all along that we've
had some good hitters and today, they
finally broke out with the hits.”*

Waigwa, Stones
Records Fall In Indoor C.

‘Special To The El Paso Times

OKLAHOMA CITY — University of
Texas at El Paso’s Wilson Waigwa shared
meet honors with world record-breaking
Dwight Stones here Saturday night at the
United States Track and Field
Federation's National Indoor Cham-
pionships.

Waigwa, a sophomore and threc-time
All-America runner, ran the second fastest
indoor mile in the world this year with a
3:57.0 clocking, 4.6 seconds ahead of
challenger Mike Slack, and breaking the
old 1972 meet record of 4:03.8 set by Tom
Von Ruden of the Pacific Coast Track
Club.

Waigwa’s performance ushered in’

another U.T. El Paso school record, bet-
tering his own 3:57.2 set last year about
this same time in San Diego.

Stones, a lanky PCC competitor, erased
his old 7-foot-5\ standard set earlier this
year at the Los Angeles Times Games, as
he cleared 7-5'2.

Other records came when Jim Bolding of
Oklahoma, set a 600-yard dash mark in the
special finals, of 1:09.2 to break David
Crowe's old 1:09.9 record of last year
(Crowe is from Louisiana Tech). Bolding
had to leave for the Toronto Games early,
and Colorado State’s Bob Barry took the
regular 600 in 1:11.0.

Kansas’s mile relay team turned in a
3:14.9 mile relay clocking to eclipse the
3:15.2 set last year by Oklahoma State and
Jayhawk Waddell Smith set a 440-yard
dash record in 48.1, bettering Dennis
Schultz of the PCTC’s old 48.3.
