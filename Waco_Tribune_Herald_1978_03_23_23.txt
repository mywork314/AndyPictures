Clifton’s Pat Olsen has made good
things happen for many of us. He is
loved and respected by all of baseball
although he never realized a goal of
being a big league pitcher,

He has attended 199 World Series
games, most All-Star games and
witnessed the induction of many
friends into baseball's hall of fame at
Cooperstown. Many have been his
guests at the World Series,

Tuesday was the biggest day of Pat
Olsen's life.

Old Texas Aggies from everywhere
joined this generation's crowd to
dedicate a new $2 million baseball
stadium in his honor. Four generations
of his family shared the tribute to Pat
and his wife Elsie ~ Edwin (Pat Jr.)
and Martha Olsen, grandson Steve and
Peggy Olsen and great-granddaughter
Stefany, So did a busload of hometown
folks headed by Mayor Charles
Isenhower. Among them was Dr. S. L.
Witcher, Baylor Athletic Hall of
Famer.

ALSO THERE were three other
members of the 1923 Aggie baseball
team, on which Pat was star piteher
~ Capt. Buggs Morris, Jack Forgason
and Stanley Chapman, Roger Simpson
was unable to attend because of illness
in the family, Hall of Fame umpire
Jocko Conlan temporarily came out of
retirement to work three innings of the
Aggie-Southern California game. The
Trojans spoiled a perfect day hy
beating Tom Chandler's Aggies on a
two-run homer and big league
prospect Bill Bordley’s four-hit
pitching. The southpaw sophomore is
4-0 for the season and 18-0 for his
career,

Trojan coach Rod Dedeaux sprang a
Surprise in the dedication ceremonies
by presenting Olsen a USC T-shirt in
Trojan colors. He also presented
school officials a serving tray
engraved with the USC seal. Dedeaux
is college baseball's biggest winner
and has sent most players to the big
leagues. Fred Lynn, Boston Red Sox
rookie of the year and MVP in 1975, is
one of his boys. His gifts delighted the
partisan Aggie crowd.

Associate Director of Athletics
Marvin Tate presided over dedication
ceremonies. Speakers were President
Jarvis E. Miller, Athletic Director and
football coach Emory Bellard, and
Chandler, who said he was always
trying to get “a little better dirt to
play on but didn’t expect a stadium
with it.”

Clyde H. Wells, chairman of the
Texas A&M board of regents,
presented the field and name to Dr.
Miller, who unveiled a bronze plaque
to place on the stadium. Olsen said in
his acceptance speech that baseball
training and self-discipline instilled by

CE. (PAT) OLSEN stadium gives
his old school one of the finest college
baseball facilities in the nation. It has
@ natural grass surface and is aligned
in a north-south direction with the
pitcher facing south, It is located
across the road from Kyle Field.
Dimensions are 330 feet down each
foul line, 370 feet to the power alleys in
ight and left-center fields and 400 feet
to center. There is a li-foot warning
track next to the outfield fence and
from first base to third base in front of
the grandstand,

The partially covered two-deck
grandstand seats approximately 5,000.

Old athletes have a never-ending
Store of delightful stories and will talk
all night if given a chance. We're
sorry we missed Conlan’s session
Monday night. He and old New York
Giant Hall of Famer Fred Lindstrom
were still going at the buffet luncheon
which almost filled a large room in the
student center. Talk got around to El
Paso sports legend Andy Cohen. Andy
was one of the Giants’ first Jewish
ballplayers. Waco Cubs’ manager Del
Pratt sold him to John McGraw for
$20,000,

In 1928, McGraw traded Rogers
Hornsby to the Boston Braves, Cohen
replaced Hornsby at second base and
was on a hitting spree while Rogers
was starting slow. New York paper
began printing batting average
comparisons of the two every day.

“Rogers said they shouldn't do it,”
said Lindstrom. “It would hurt the
kid.” That probably had nothing to do
with it but Hornsby batted .387 with 21
home runs. Cohen had a good year
with 274.
