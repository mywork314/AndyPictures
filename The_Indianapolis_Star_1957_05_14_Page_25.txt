By MAX GREENWALD

Bat Boy Night will be ob-
served tonight at Victory
Field,

Johnnie Bencik of Cathedral
Hizh School, winner of The
Indianapolis Star’s 1957 bat
boy contest, will be honored
between games of tonight's
doubleheader.

The twin bill between the
Indianapolis Indians and
Charleston, starting at 6:30
o'clock, was forced because of
postponement due to rain of
last night’s tilt.

Jep Cadou, Jr., sports editor
of The Star, will present a
watch to Bencik in the be-
tween-game ceremonies. Broth-
er Pedro, C.S.C., Cathedral
principal, also will participate
in the program.

OTHER FACULTY members
and Cathedral students will be
on hand to join in paying
tribute to Bencik. Contestants
in the sixth annual event will
attend as guests of Indians,
Inc.

Indianapolis is awaiting de-
velopments as player help is
expected from the Chicago
White Sox.

The Sox yesterday released
Ellis Kinder, veteran pitcher,
and must drop two more play-
ers by midnight tomorrow to
reach the 25-man limit.

Ed Szyczewski and Bill
DuFour probably will be Man-
ager Andy Cohen’s pitching
choices tonight.

“I'm not crazy about these
rainouts,” Cohen said. “Our
pitchers need the work.”

The Tribe pilot has ordered
a workout for his pitchers,
catchers and infielders this

morning at 10 o'clock, if
weather conditions permit.

Szyczewski has a 2-0 record,
and DuFour's is 1-1. Neither
has a decision against Charles-
ton.

Hal Woodeshick, the big
southpaw who went the route
in defeating the Indians May 4
although he allowed 13 hits,
was named by Frank Skaff,
Charleston manager, for the
seven-inning first game. Woode-
shick had seven strikeouts in
the tilt at Charleston.

The other hurler for the Sen-
ators will be John Tsitouris,
who was batted from the
mound by the Tribe May 3 in
the fourth inning.

Two new pitchers were
with the Senators here yes-
. They are Bob Shaw
and Larry Donovan, trans-
ferred from Toronto. In this
deal, the parent Detroit club
sent Pitcher Jack Crimian to
the Maple Leafs. Charleston
also received Outfielder Bill
Wilson in the trade.

Convening in a special meet-
ing yesterday at Chicago, di-
rectors of the other seven
American Association clubs
promised help to Louisville.

An announcement after the
meeting said ‘all directors ex-
pressed desire to arrange as-
signment of two or more play-
ers to Louisville within a day
or so,”

The last-place Colonels, now
10 games out of first place,
went into receivership last
year. The league took care of
receiver's claims and turned
over the club's assets free to
a Louisville group.

Player help would be sold
or optioned to Louisville by
association members or by
their major league parent
