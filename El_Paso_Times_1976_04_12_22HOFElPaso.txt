Three Minds Began Hall Of Fame

By FLIP MORIN
‘Times Sports Writer

It was 21 years ago that
Chuck Whitlock, Bob Ingram
and John Phelan conjured up
the El Paso Athletic Hall of
Fame idea. For that first en-
shrinement they only had two
categories — living and
posthumous athlete.

Sports around El Paso has
naturally grown and so has the
Hall of Fame. This year (7:30
p.m. Tuesday in the Civic
Center) the Eastwood High
School basketball team,
University of Texas at El Paso
track teams plus seven in-
dividual athletes will be
honored. The newest category,
sports media, will find
Whitlock joining Ingram and
Phelan on the list of honorees.

The whole thing came about
in 1955 as Whitlock (El Paso
Times), Ingram (Herald Post)
and Phelan (KTSM Radio)
were rapping in the Press Club
of those days, located under
what is now Joe Borzi's at
Kansas and Franklin. “The
city already had its Gridiron
Shows which spoofed civic and
sports figures," Whitlock said.
“So we thought it was time for
El Paso to recognize our
athletes with something. The
Texas School of Mines had its

annual banquet, but that was
about all.”

They came up with the Hall
of Fame idea, and contacted
Chris Fox of the State National
Bank, who could always be
counted on to advance
noteworthy El Paso projects,
They were on their way.

“That first year we inducted
Andy Cohen,” Whitlock said.
“We held all our banquets at
the old Hilton, which is now the
Plaza Hotel,” Whitlock recalls,
“Incidently, Andy is a one-of-a-
kind major league baseball
manager because he is the only
person to never lose a game,”

It seems Cohen was a coach
for the Phillies back in 1960.
The club manager, Eddie
Sawyer, had lost a game to
Cincinnati the day before, so he
up and quit. Cohen was called
to manage the team and they
beat Milwaukee in 10 innings.
“So Andy is in the books as
being the only major league
manager to have a perfect won-
loss record," Whitlock said.

“He was our first inductee,
along with N. A. Ferguson."

The second banquet turned
out to be a blooper for the trio,
as Margaret Varner, a world-
chess tennis and squash player,
was their honoree. “We held
the enshrinement the same
night as the annual Gridiron
Show was staged,” Whitlock
said. “In those days Polly
Harris and Art Leibson were
the ramrods, and they really
came up with some zippy
material. We went to the show
after our sports banquet and
Margaret, her parents and
friends walked out.”

The Gridiron shows were
traditionally ‘“‘stag’’ events,
and almost anything went in
those days.

“Sports in those first years
consisted of golf, baseball and
the Golden Gloves boxing,”
Whitlock pointed out, “There
wasn't any racing and the
School of Mines had football.
There were only four high
schools.”

One of the most em-
barrassing banquets was in
1959. ‘Our guest speaker was
Frank Leahy, coach at Notre
Dame,” he said. “Only he
didn't show up until late in the
evening. He went to the Airport
Hilton instead of the downtown
Hilton, that was the year Jack
Harden was inducted."

One year golf professional
Bob Rosburg didn't show up at
ail to address the guests.

“It has grown to really
something now," Whitlock

noted. ‘The only thing missing,
and we hope someone comes up
with an idea soon, is for a
permanent hall to display all
the photos and ac-
complishments of these fine
athletes we have in El Paso.”

The first Hall of Fame was a
sellout, as was the 1961 event
honoring Willie Shoemaker, the
winningest jockey ever, from
Fabens. The old Press Club is
long gone but sports nostalgia
will be re-kindled Tuesday,
sellout or not.
