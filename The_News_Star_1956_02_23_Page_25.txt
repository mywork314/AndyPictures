Andy Cohen Back
For 2nd Season
With N.O. Pels

NEW ORLEANS, Feb. 23 (7 —|
Andy Cohen, preparing to start his |
second season as manager of the
New Orleans Pelicans, learned the
hard way that the baseball season
isn't over until the last out.

Cohen had his team in the South-
ern Association first division for
152 of 154 games last season. On
the last day the Pelicans dropped
from fourth to sixth place.

Cohen came to New Orleans aft-
er leading Denver of the Western
League to two pennants and two
second place finishes during four
years as manager there.

Cohen had a long and varied
baseball career as player, man-
ager, scout and manager again.

A three-sport standout at the
University of Alabama, he left
school in 1925 at the end of his
junior year. He signed his first
professional contract that year
with Waco, then in the Texas
League.

Before the season was over the
New York Giants bought Cohen’s
contract for the then fabulous sum
of $25,000. But the Giants didn’t
need any young infielders then.
They had Rogers Hornsby at sec-
ond and Travis Jackson at short-
stop.

So it was 1928 before Cohen got
his first chance with New York.
In the meantime, he played for
Buffalo in the International
League. While there, he set an all-
time league fielding record for sec-
ond basemen. He also had a .353
batting average one season.

He didn’t last long in the major
leagues, After three seasons he
was sent to Newark and then to
Indianapolis.

A few years later he started his
managerial career at Pine Bluff,
Ark. He went to Dayton, Ohio, next
and then Elmira.

World War II interrupted his
baseball career. When he was dis-
charged, he took over as manager-
owner of El Paso, Tex. He held
that job two seasons, 1945-46. The
next year he was a scout for the
Boston Braves of the Nationai
League.

In 1948 he resumed his) career
as a manager. This time he was
at Eau Claire, Wis. He stayed
there until 1950 when he took over
at Denver.
