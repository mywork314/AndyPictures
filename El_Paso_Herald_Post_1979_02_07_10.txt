Super stars?
|

EDITOR: I am glad I read what a
sportswriter from the morning news-
paper wrote about two super-stars
that played baseball with UTEP

New we know why Coach Andy
Cohen never had a winning team or a
winning season. No coach in this
world can possibly win if he has to put
up with one wet-nose super-star, let
alone two or three.

Here are two prima-donnas, just out
of high school and junior college (big
deal) telling this sportswriter that
Cohen was a lousy coach. A fine coach
with more than 40 years of baseball
know-how.

These super-stars will never reach
the minors, much less the majors with
that know-it-all attitude and their nar-
row minds,

They will always be fighting their
tails off against their coaches and
never against their opponents.

Who, in his right mind, can tell
others that the coach is wrong and not
follow his instructions, his teaching?
These two super-star stars have to
learn to obey, concentrate and re-
spect their parents, teachers, coaches
and later their bosses,

} am sure these two super-stars will
never amount to anything. How can
they, knowing more than Cohen, a
man that spend forty years in base-
ball and knows that there is still lots to
learn?

Larry McFarlin is a fine young
coach and I already feel sorry for
him. He will remember that article
written about these two super-stars, I
am sure and he will be in the same
boat Andy Cohen was for twenty
years as a coach.

His (Larry's) downfall will be from
within, if he tolerates the wet-nose
super-stars, like Andy did.

Cohen played and coached in the
minors and major leagues for forty
years and won the respect of lot of
people for all these and for his true
and loyal heart he has.

I wish I could live long enovgh to see
if these two super-stars can match
that. — Lionel Forti, Box 26631.
