For ‘Krush,’

the figures

didn't lie

WHEN THE U.T. El Paso Miners
played Colorado State Saturday night
in the Special Events Center, 20 years
of statistical work for the school in
football and basketball were com-
pleted by 0.C, Krushwitz.

Having written down enough
figures to fill several books, Krush”
decided to hang up his calculator, his
slide rule, percentage tables, pencil
sharpener and other tools of his trade,

There should have been more than
just a simple announcement. He
should have been presented a cake
with .750 on it instead of candles, That
could be Krush’s batting average over
20 years, One of .1000 would be better
but Krush would be the first to say
that nobody's perfect and no baseball
player ever hit as much as .750 over a
20-year span.

Krush retired on his own volition.
He said in his job as a real estate ap-
praiser with C.W. Lyons and
Associates, he's compelled to travel a
good deal,

It was a difficult decision to make
since Krush has been allied closely
with the school for so long a time. He
was a mathematics teacher at the
school for several years and for seven
years helped Andy Cohen coach the
Miner baseball team.

Stat man’s delight

KRUSHWITZ WAS prepared for
the opening game of the 1965 football
season at UTEP. It marked the debut
of Bobby Dobbs as head coach.

The Miners opened with North
‘Texas in the Sun Bowl. ‘‘I knew there
would be a lot of action in the game,”
Krush said. “Prior to it the Miners
held an intrasquad scrimmage and
there were something like 80 passes
thrown,”

Bill Stevens threw for a touchdown
‘on the first Miner play from serim-
mage, Krush remembers. He and his
statistical crew weren't disappointed,
They kept busy jotting down all the
passes Stevens threw in amassing 500
aerial yards as the Miners won 61-15.

Coaching aids

KRUSH KEEPS statistics in their
proper perspective. He doesn’t think
they are the most important aspects
of athletics.

He admits they can be deceiving.
Some teams wind up with more first
downs and more yardage than their
opponents yet lose. It has been said
that statistics are for losers,

Krush does believe statistics are
helpful coaches. They do give any
coach a pretty good line on his players
and team “‘tendency"' which seems to
bea big thing in scouting,

The knottiest problem Krush has
had in his career as a statistician
came in the Arizona-Auburn Sun Bow!
game in 1962,

Arizona had two fourth down punts,
One of the punts was blocked. The ball
bounded back to the Arizona punter.
He got it away this time for about 15
or 20 yards. Krush's decision was to
credit Arizona with two punts. One
was a team punt and the other was
given to the player who kicked the
ball twice,

“You've got to be objective to be a
good statistician,” said Krush ‘You
should be fair to all the athletes."

In other words, don't be a “homer.”
