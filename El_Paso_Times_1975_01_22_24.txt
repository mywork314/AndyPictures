Miller Leaves Empty Hole

Miner Baseball Team Works Toward Opener

By MARK LEIBSON
‘Times Sports Writer

Baseball season for The
University of Texas at El Paso
hasn't even started yet and
already, Manager Andy Cohen
is confronted with a serious
problem.

Seems last summer, second
baseman Mark Miller was
involved in a baseball program
in the Dallas area and he
caught the eye of the Texas
Rangers.

Before Miller returned to El
Paso, the Rangers made sure
Miller’s career with the Miners
was finished. Texas signed the
slick-fielding Eastwood High
School graduate to a
professional baseball contract
and next month, Miller will be

SRR ON

heading toward spring training
instead of swinging a bat for
U-T. El Paso.

“Dam, I hate to lose Miller,””
said Cohen Tuesday as he
starts his 13th year at the helm.
“Miller is a great ball player
and I was counting on him
heavily.”

But, that’s the way of life and
Cohen knows it.

So, as the Miners workout
towards the start of the season -
Feb. 14 and 15th with
doubleheaders against Texas
Tech at Dudley Field, Cohen
must find a replacemient for
Miller at second.

“There's going to be a tough
battle for secondbase between
Art Bravo, Terry Hudson and
Alan Morris, a graduate from

Eastwood who transferred
from Davis Junior College,”
said Cohen. Bravo is a fresh-
man right out of Austin High
School while Morris ranks a
junior in classification.

While the diamondmen
worked out at Hugh Meyer
Field on their hitting, Cohen
was predicting an improved
team from last season's 24-26
squad which also posted a 2-16
Western Athletic Conference
mark.

“We should have a better all
around ball club,” Cohen
prognosticated. “Our defense
is just as good and if our pit-
ching comes around, we should
be doing all right. Pitching is
going to have to carry us this
year.”

Two hurlers Cohen is
counting on to fill some big
shoes are a pair of lefthanders
in George Landreth from
Ranger JC and freshman Ron
Wrobel, a 17-year-old from
Chicago. Says Cohen, ‘They're
both tremendous prospects and
they could be what we're
looking for to improve our
pitching staff.”

Cohen has a fine corp of
returning fielders in third
baseman Glenn Villarreal,
Terry Dilingham at first and
Joe Pennies roaming around in
center field.

Villarreal, as well as Pen-
nies, played in all Sl of the
Miner games and accumulated
.383 and .323 batting averages
respectively. Dillingham only
saw action in 39 contests but
was the third leading hitter
with a .324 average with four
homeruns. He also led U.T. El
Paso with a .987 fielding per-

centage.

“Both Glen and Joe Pennies
have improved steadily and I
feel that both players are
professional prospects,’’
praised Cohen. “Dillingham
will also be a big asset to our
team.”

Dillingham could see com-
petition from John Hahn, a 240-
pound center for U.T. El Paso
during the football season. The
big catcher-first baseman is
determined to become a starter
and with all that weight behind
his swing, Cohen might not
have to think twice about
sticking Hahn into the lineup.

Other returning starters
include shortstop Dave Nance,
a walk on who never played
baseball before joining the
Miners last season, Jimmy
Walsh, a right fielder who hails
out of Burges and Keith
Rushing who plays both left
and right field.
