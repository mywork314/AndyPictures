Visions of Fifth
Fade for Indians

Speciol to The News

WICHITA — The Indianap-
olis Indians lost their self-
appointed commission as
spoilers in the American As-
sociation and also their hopes
for fifth place here last night.

The Indians, who beat the
Wichita Braves five straight
times in Indianapolis last
week to revive the pennant
race, helped rebury it here by
dropping 3-1 and 7-5 decisions
to the league leaders.

Manager Andy Cohen's
crew still has three games
left with Wichita, but the
Braves have a 214-game lead
over Denver with only three
games to play.

Thus, the only chance the
Indians have for spoiling now
is mathematical. And that’s
the same kind of shot they

have at fifth place. They trail
Omaha by two games in the
loss column with three games
to play.

OLD INDIAN
STORY UNFOLDS

Last night was an old story
for the Indians. They just
couldn’t match their hitting
and pitching. They set some
kind of a record by getting at
least one hit in each of the
first 13 innings they batted,
and were set down in order
only twice all night.

Vince Garcia collected sey-
en hits and Charley Willioms had
tive, but Neil Chrisley, Jim Del-
sing, and Ron Jackson — the
towers of power—went ]-for-22.
Jackson struck out five times in
eight trips and hit the ball out
of the infield only once,

Chrisley injured his foot in
the first game, and saw only
pinch-hitting duty in the
nightcap. Cohen said he
thought Neil would be back
in the lineup tonight.

The Braves took the seven-
inning opener as Carlton Wil-

ley won his 21st game in a
duel with Jim McDonald.

Harry Hankebrink’s 234
homer with a man on in the
third was the payoff blow,
but McDonald deserved a
better fate. He rationed out
only six hits, and all three
Wichita runs were the direct
result of mental and mechan-
ical errors.

Hanebrink's homer was set up
when Garcia was slow covering
second on a double play ball,
ond the third Wichita run came
after Jackson dropped a perfect
throw from McDonald.

Jim Landis had his best
batting day since being sent
down by the White Sox in
the nightcap as he hit two
home runs.
