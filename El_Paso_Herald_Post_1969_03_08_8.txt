Hunt, who lost his opener
this season to the University
of Albuquerque, had the plea-
sure of coming back and
going the distance in the first
game against Tech at Dudley
Field in weather more fit for
football than baseball.

MILNER, WHO didn’t get
fielding support in a game
against New Mexico State Uni-
versity this week, looked as if
he was in for he same tr
ment early in the second
game. But he kept his cool
and brought his team home
for the win.

Coach Andy Cohen said, “I
was real pleased with pitching
performance of both Hunt and
Milner. They hung in there
real tough despite some shoddy
fielding.”

Hunt was in trouble only |

two times in the game, in the
second and fifth innings.

In the second inning he
began getting behind on the
batters and then had to give
them the fat pitch to hit.

He made them hit though,
on the ground, into a team that
was battling them with cold
fingers: The Miners backed
him well by stopping the
Raiders’ chance to score with
@ tag out at home.

MILNER got off to a slow

start in the second game with

Tech getting two unearned |
runs in the first inning off the |

lefty. But after that he got
stronger as he went along in
the effort.

“Milner didn’t get mad at
anybody in that first inning
when Tech got those two
unearned runs on balls that
never left the infield," Cohen
said.

He added, “Those boys who
hurt him the worst in the first
inning with errors were the
ones who came through with
key hits and runs in the fifth
inning.”

Milner held the Raiders to
only three hits and struck out
six besides going the distance.

Mike Ward was the man
who led the Miners at the plate
in both games. He collected a
total of three hits for both
games including a double and
two RBIs. Hunt also helped his
own cause in the first game
with a double and two RBIs.
