Distraction
Hurt Marc
At Arizona

TUCSON — When Andy Coh-
en’s U.T. El Paso Miner base-
ball team met the University
of Arizona Wildcats today in
the final two games of the
year here he hoped to have his
pitchers do more concentrat-
ing.

His ace Marc Bombard suf-
fered defeat when Arizona got
to him in the first four innings
for seven runs and a 7-4 win
last night.

“Bombard was getting
behind on his batters and not
concentrating on what he was
going to throw. Their pitcher,
Rich Hinton, who hurt us the
worst is a good example. Bom-
bard had two strikes and no
balls on him when Hinton hit a
three-run triple.

HINTON ALSO added a
homer to his list of runs batted
in and hits for the day.

Cohen called on Larry Smith
in the fourth to relieve Bom-
bard. “Smith came in and
pitched beautiful ball,” Cohen
said. ‘‘Even when I took him
out, the Arizona fans applaud-
ed his performance.”

“Smith threw a good fast
ball and a tantalizing curve
ball,” he added.

COHEN LOST another one
of his outfielders yesterday to
leg injuries. The Miners, who
have been plagued with
injured outfielders all season
now have centerfielder Bobby
Morales out. Morales suffered
a pulled hamstring muscle
when he was running the
bases.

Cohen said, ‘‘The difference
in the game was their long ball
hitting. Our boys played well
and fought all the way, but
Arizona bats were too strong.”

John Miller was the big bat
for the Miners with two singles
and a triple. Danny Blanco
added to the cause with two
RBI's.
