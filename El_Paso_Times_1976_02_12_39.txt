All Broken Up About It

WHILE THINGS ARE looking up
for the office softball team — our
centerfielder promised to remember
to bring his glove this weekend — that
isn’t to say hope springs eternal on
every diamond.

And speaking of stones, a few have
been cast lately in the direction of the
University of Texas at El Paso
baseball program. Life for the
Miners, it seems, isn’t what it used to
be. Or what the players feel it should
be.

One of their number called yours,
truly here the other day to alert us to
the fact that paper plates get better
treatment than Miner baseball
players are getting.

“Every bat we have is broken and
the balls we’re using are all taped,”
said the voice. “‘And now we've been
told we won't get to play all our games
at Dudley Field.”

Our first reaction was to ask the guy
what soap ‘opera script he was
reading from. I mean, although the
Miners don’t deserve the same fringe
benefits the Cincinnati Reds have
coming, they would seem to be en-
titled to the same breaks as what little
league teams get. Surely, a team that

will be asked later on in the season to
“play ball’ against the likes of
Arizona State and Arizona deserves a
little in the way of equipment.

As we were to discover, however,
the sad story is a true story, at least in
part. The bats are broken, all right.
The balls are taped and Dudley Field
won't be theirs to have and to hold this
season. But it’s not as if the athletic
department is trying to be mean to the
baseball players the way Lucy is to
Charlie Brown.

It’s a fact that money is creating
more problems for the Miners than
any curve ball pitcher they’ve ever
faced. Turning UTEP into a national
powerhouse in college baseball isn’t a
priority because the climate isn’t
right. The financial climate, that is.
