The Tolan Tale

_ DANDY DON MEREDITH says there are 10 foreign-born
field goal kickers in the NFL. Asked how to defense Garo

Yepremian and the other soccer-style foreign-born kickers,

Norm Van Brocklin of the Atlanta Falcons said, “tighten the
immigration laws.” Bobby Tolan played for the Tulsa Oilers
in 1964. They were in the Texas League then and we saw
them play against the Sun Kings at Dudley Field. Tolan was
a speedster who hit .297 for Tulsa. He looked like a “couldn't
miss” big league prospect. The Pirates evidently didn’t think
he was or they would not have let him get away to the St.
Louis Cardinals by the draft. The Cards traded him to
Cincinnati for Vada Pinson and with the Reds, he hit .305 in
1970 and .303 in 1971, led the National League in stolen
bases in 1970 with 57. He sat out the 1972 season with a leg
injury. This year he slumped to .206 and had some club
house hassles that led to his suspension. He'll miss the
playoffs, and the World Series even if the Reds make it.
They'll probably trade him during the winter. I’m sorry to
hear that Boomer Nunez, one of El Paso’s most colorful
sports figures, was decked with a heart attack. I'm glad to
hear that he’s improving. Andy Cohen tells about the
baseball game his Miners played against the New Mexico
State Aggies. No umpires showed up. Presley Askew, the Ag
coach, agreed to umpire. He called balls and strikes and
plays and coached the Aggie team, too. The Miners won 2-1.
