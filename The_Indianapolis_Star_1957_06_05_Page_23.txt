By MAX GREENWALD
Barry Latman established a season high in strikeouts
for Indianapolis pitchers last night as the Tribe con-

quered St. Paul, 7-3.

Latman fanned 10 batters
and received 11-hit swatting
support, three by Neil Chrisley.

The Indians got to Glenn Mc-
Minn for five safeties in the
first inning to build up a four-
run margin.

Southpaw Chuck Templeton
took up relief work with two
out in the opening frame.

A bases-full sacrifice fly by
Latman accounted for a run in
the third, while Run Jackson's
long triple to right center in
the sixth sent across the other
two markers, -

LATMAN allowed only five
hits in eight innings with
Granny Gladstone's first home
run of the year, a clout over the

left-field bricks, accounting for |

a tally in the second.

Gladstone singled over a run
in the sixth and Jack Spears
drove over one in the ninth
as the Apostles raised their hit
total to eight.

The series with St. Paul will
close tonight when Railroad
Night will be observed. The
event is sponsored by the Rail-
road Community Committee
and there will be a pre-game
program beginning at 7:30
o'clock.

During the ceremonies, the
Tribesmen will wear engineer
caps and the managers—Andy
Cohen of Indianapolis and Max
Macon of the Saints—will be
presented with gifts.

HIT-AND RUN—St. Paul has
been hit hard by injuries and

bad start when Spears singled
and Dick Young walked, But
the next three Saints couldn't
get the ball out of the infield.

The Indians argued with
Umpire Bob Stewart about a
play at first base in the third,
contending that Young's throw
on Don Prohovich's sacrifice
pulled Spears off the bag.

Orin Ray is umpiring in an
Indianapolis series for the
first time. He is from the Wes-
tern League and is working
in place of Dick Phillips, ho
is ill.
