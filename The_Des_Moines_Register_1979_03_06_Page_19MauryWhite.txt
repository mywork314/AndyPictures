ONCE UPON A TIME, and it was
in 1926 when the Giants were in New
York, a fellow named Babe Ruth was
packing in fans for the rival Yankees
and Giant management was
desperate for a good drawing card.

Because of the large Jewish popu-
lation of the city, the Giants searched
high and low for a talented Jew who
might build a large local following,
even if not able to smite as many
home runs as the Bambino.

Lo and behold, they latched ahold
of an infielder in the minors, Andy
Cohen, and brought him up in mid-
season. In those days, before public
address systems, a man with a
megaphone made announcements
from the home plate area.

Anxious to get about establishing
Cohen, the management promptly
sent him in the day he reported, to
pinch hit for a popular Irish infielder,
Joseph Doyle.

“And now, subbing for Joseph
Doyle and appearing at the Polo
Grounds for the first time .. . Cohen!
Andy Cohen!” yelled the man with the
megaphone.

A bulky, redfaced Irisher, of the
type who might take a drink,
promptly stood up in the stands
behind home plate and bellowed back,
even louder:

“and now ... leaving the Polo
Grounds for the last time is me ...
O'Grady! Michael 0’Grady!”

Cohen hit 14 home runs and .281 in
three major league seasons and did
not take much interest away from
Ruth. Years later, he was a popular
figure here as Denver manager
during the post-war heydey of the
Three-I League.
