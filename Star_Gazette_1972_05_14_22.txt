By AL MALLETTE

Kip Horsburgh and Carl Fazio still
maintain their motto ‘100,000 or Bust’’ for
Elmira Pioneer baseball 1972 but the goal now
may be just a bit out of reach.

Elmira has had four plus-100,000 gate
seasons and a bit of research points to two
distinct ingredients needed to cash in
100,00— excellent spring weather plus ex-
citing players.

Elmira 1972 hasn't had the spring
weather and with so many rainouts, no one
has been able to establish himself as an ex-
citing player.

Elmira’s first 100,000-plus gate season
Fo 1939 — a very unusual year, weather-
wise.

The Pioneers didn’t have a rainout the
first 23 games (the season opener was played
in a drizzle) and they had such sparklers as
Pete Reiser (who later injured his arm),
George Staller, John Hvisdos and Bill
MeWilliams.

Of course, you must remember, too, that
all 1939 games up to mid-June were played in
the afternoon when it’s Decidedly warmer
than it is at night and more conducive to
playing, sitting and watching.

Elmira attracted 124,383 in 1941 — and
again the Pioneers had fantastically fine
weather in the spring, only two rainouts from
season-opening day, April 23, through
Memorial Day, May 30.

That year, too, Johnny Ogden had some
real heroes in Elmira uniforms — like Eddie
Robinson, Andy Cohen, Franke Madura,
Eddie Kobesky, Sal Maglie and Ray Roche.

War year 1943 saw more good spring
weather plus such crowd-pleasers as Alex
Mustaikis, Sad Sam Zoldak, Ed Busch, Don
Smith and Les Moss.

Spence Harris began 1951 with an Art
Fabbro one-hitter before more than 5,000
opening night fans — and the fans never
stopped coming as an all-time record 126,238

_ paraded through the Dunn Field gates to

watch Don Zimmer, Bill Boudreau, Gale
Wade, Nap Peeples, Bob Wilson and Ed
Roebuck.

Elmira hasn’t hit the 100,000 gate figure
since and the miserable weather (who can
remember when we've had a worse first three
weeks of baseball?) this year has put a
