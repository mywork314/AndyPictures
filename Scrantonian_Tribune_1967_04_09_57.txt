‘Shanty’ Hogan Loved Steak
Played Vaudeville in City

Man mountain Francis ‘‘Shanty” Hogan who caught for
the New York Giants, ate steak at breakfast, and played
winter vaudeville in Scranton with his Hebrew teammate
Andy Cohen, is dead at 61, reviving memories of the late
20s and early 30s when the huge New. Englander was at the
peak of his popularity.

Hogan and Cohen came here on a week-long schedule
and drew almost as many fans when “Shanty’’ sat. down to
eat as they did with their cleverly written skit, containing
parodies of popular songs,

Sentimental as he was massive Hogan insisted on visit-
ing the Cathedral Cemetary, Moscow, resting place of the
late Hugh Jennings. It was zeroish cold, and the snow-
banks were almost forboding, yet they went and bareheaded
Hogan recited a prayer for the Hall of Fame member he
knew as a Giants’ coach in his first year after being ar
by the Boston Braves.

A visit to “Shanty’s” Somerville, Mass., home was
equally as rewarding. There his angelic mother always had
the steak and eggs her James Francis loved after arising.

It was the same story wherever Hogan played during 13
major league seasons. He had the best batting average at
the table, and always knew where they served the finest,
and biggest platters of porterholse, filet mignon and such.
