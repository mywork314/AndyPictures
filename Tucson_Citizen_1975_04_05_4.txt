FLAGSTAFF —. Gov. Raul
Castro will appoint a citizens’
committee to investigate ac-
cess problems on state lands.

The promise came during
the Arizona Wildlife Federa-
tion’s 52nd annual convention
here today.

Castro said the committee
will be composed of ranchers
and farmers, plus hunters,
hikers and environmentalists.

“Both sides must realize
their responsibilities, but the

ranchers must realize they
have a responsibility to the
public and that the public has
a right to enter state land.”

Castro said he would not
announce his appointment to
the Arizona Game and Com-
mission until next week be-
cause “more than 150 persons
from every corner of the state
have applied.”

He has not had time to re-
view all the applicants, he
said.

“It’s hard for a pitcher to
Stay up all the way in a game
like that,” Kindall observed.
“But Craig did a fine job. And
he’s cemented himself as our
No. 1 starter.”

UA center fielder Dave
Stegman set the pace when he
led off the first inning for the
Cats with an inside-the-park
home run, thoroughly demor-
alizing UTEP hurler George
Landreth, who came in with a
0.85 earned run average.

The Cats toyed with Lan-
dreth in the first inning with
just two runs, then exploded
for eight runs in the second —
Mike Gatlin’s double driving
in two runs.

Landreth had already de-
parted by that time, but the
Cats were no more friendly to
his successor, Bob Knox, and
struck for eight more runs in
the third.

From then on, it was a
matter of which would come
first — the end of the game or
sunrise.

UA shortstop Bill Simpson
ended up driving home four

triple, Pat O'Sullivan, Steg-
man and Ken Bolek each fur-
nished the Cats with three
RBI's apiece.
