We Get? One, That's All

"By LESTER KOELLING
| There’s one thing ‘about get-
ting drubbed 18-1; anything
you do thereafter will be an
improvement,

With that in mind, the In-
dianapolis Indians will be in a
vengeful mood tonight when
they face the Denver Bears in
the series finale. They will
need to be to stay in fifth
place and in position to regain
fourth during a_ three-game
series with Omaha, starting at
2:30 tomorrow and to the ac-
companiment of The News
Newsboys’ Band.

The Bears did everything but
steal a base last night, and they
didn’t need to do that, as they
administered the most humili-
ating defeat of the season to the
Indians. Their 20 hits were of
every known variety—bloops and
blasts, dribblers and bad bouncers,
It was, without doubt, the Bears’
night to howl.

Through it all, the Indians
kept their heads up; they never
quit trying, But the massacre

Some third-base fans ap-

making caustic suggestions to
Manager Andy Cohen and the
Indians. One of them cracked
“Why did’ya trip him, Andy?”
when Jose Valdivielso slipped
and fell in the mud while
rounding third base. Another
reminded Cohen that he had
seen a track meet in the after-
noon and had hoped to see a
“baseball game tonight.”

Those remarks and incidents
failed to stir the humor
in the Indians, who were feel-
ing badly enough in becoming
an unwilling punching bag for
other American Association
clubs. Just a week before they
served as sparring partners for
the lowly Louisville Colonels.
Later, Minneapolis got on its
feet at the expense of the Tribe
after losing three to St, Paul,
and now Denver,

The Bears hadn‘t won three
straight all season until last
night; they hadn’t scored more
than 13 runs until last night, and
hadn't obtained more than 18
hits until last night. In fact, few
recalled the Bears being in the
league until they came to Indian-
apolis,

Someone suggested that the
Bears were due for an outburst
like last night; that they were
a far better club than the rec-
ords indicate.

“We were just due to ex-
plode,” said a visiting writer.
“Our pitchers had allowed an
earned run average of 2.16 in
eight games before coming
here, yet we won only two
games, We were just due.”

True as the observation may
have been, it somehow lacked
a comforting affect to listeners.

Coach Proves Who's
Tops in Tennis Game

In tennis, as in other sports,
the coach usually has the last
word, and that’s the way it
turned out yesterday in the
final of the G.I. Singles at
Riverside. Roland Leverenz,

Tech High School coach,
whipped Clint Parsons, Butler
University’s captain, 8-6, 5-7,
6-4, for the title.
