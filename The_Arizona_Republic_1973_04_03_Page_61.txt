STEVE WESTON

Great race
... for 2nd

FOUR BASEBALL teams begin the Western Athletic Con-
ference Southern Division pennant chase this week.

And it looks like a great race

. . For second place!

Not that any of the other teams are already conceding
the title to Arizona State's Sun Devils. But the chances of
Arizona, New Mexico or Texas-El Paso actually dethroning
the powerful Sun Devils would seem to hover between slim
and none.

“They (ASU) should legitimately be classified as the favor-
ites.” understated rookie coach Jerry Kindall of Arizona,

Most observers will concede that coach Jim Brock's Devils
of 1973 are not as good (is that an accurate description?) as
his team last year,

But it would appear A-State still is that much better than
its Southern Division rivals.

Actually, the race could be over quickly.

The Sun Devils and Wildcats meet in Tucson Thursday,
Friday and Saturday. An ASU sweep—as has been the norm
of late—could start the Sun Devils towards a second consecu-
tive undefeated swing through the Southern Division.

New Mexico is enjoying a winning season—coach Bob
Leigh’s Lobos have a terrific record at this point. Still, New
Mexico’s material shouldn't match up to ASU's.

Coach Andy Cohen's UTEP Miners are winning (15-10). In
fact, he might have more players than he’s had in recent
years.

But to consider UTEP a title contender at this point is un-
imaginable.

More likely. Arizona will be the chief contender . . . so
what else is new?

“We've got to play our very best in order to win it,”’ said
Kindall, whose Wildcats take a 21-11 record into the ASU
series,

“We can’t afford a bad game or a bad series. If we are at
our best game after game, then we have a chance, We have
the kind of team that his to scratch, claw, dig and execute
in order to win,”

Pitching (isn’t it always?) is again the Wildcats’ problem
when it comes to butting heads with the Sun Devils.

“Right now,” said Kindall, “we have five guys from whom
we can pick (to pitch against ASU). I will pick the pitchers
as early in the week as I can.

“But right now, I don’t think I can draw any conclusions
so far as our pitching is concerned.”

To have any hopes of winning the division race, it would
appear UofA would have to win at least two of the three games
in Tucson.

“I'm not setting any goals for our guys,” said Kindall. “I
won't tell them they have to sweep ASU... . or even take
two of the three.

“Of course, I would be disappointed if we did worse than
win two.”

SALISBURY, N.C. — The
14th annual award dinner of
the National Sportscasters
and Sportswriters Association
will be held tonight at Cataw-
ba College.

National winners in the
writing and broadcasting
fields of sports coverage will
be announced at the dinner.
Previously announced state
winners, chosen by their col-
leagues, also will receive
awards.

The four finalists in the na-
tional sportscaster voting are
Curt Gowdy, NBC; Keith
Jackson, ABC; Ray Scott,
CBS and Hughes Sports Net-
work, and Chris Schenkel,
ABC.

The sportswriter finalists
are Melvin Durslag, Los An-
geles Times: Joe Falls, De-
troit Free Press; Jim Mur-
ray, Los Angeles Times, and
Red Smith, New York Times.

A full day of activity begins
today with a breakfast for the
guests, at which the speaker
will be Enos Slaughter, Duke
baseball coach and longtime
major league player.

Clinies will be conducted by
Byron Nelson and Sharron
Moran in golf; Ton Trabert in
tennis; Jesse Owens in track
Micki King in diving, and
Gordie Howe in Hockey.

A highlight of the dinner
will be the induction into the
NSSA Hall of Fame of “Red”
Barber and the late Arch
Ward.

Barber, who now lives in
Tallahassee, Fla., for more
than three decades, was play
- by - play announcer for the
Cincinnati Reds, Brooklyn
Dodgers and New York Yan-
kees.

Ward, for more than 20
years sports editor of the
Chicago Tribune, originated
the All-Star baseball and foot-
ball games.
