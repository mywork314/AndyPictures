Big 8 Off to Another Good Start—

Cohen to Speak
At Boys Opening

Andy Cohen will be guest
speaker at formal ceremonies
launching the 19th season for
Boys Baseball of El Paso at
6:15 p.m. tomorrow at Memo-
rial Park.

Cohen, who has been in
baseball in one capacity or
another for over 30 years, at
one time played in the Major
Leagues and is presently
coach of the U.T. El Paso
baseball team.

ALSO TAKING part in the
ceremonies will be Larry
Welsh, commmissioner of
Boys Baseball; Art Ranew,
executive director of the pro-
gram, and Al Sepkowitz, his
assistant; Rev. Harland M.
Irvin Jr., who will give the
invocation; and Gene Stogner,
athletic director for the City
Parks and Recreation Depart-
ment, who will act as master
of ceremonies.

This year’s Boys Baseball
program will include 96 teams
and will provide some 1,500
boys with the opportunity to
play organized baseball.

After the brief formal open-
ing ceremonies, there will be
two exhibition games.

All teams in the program
have been asked to arrive at
Memorial Park at 5:45 p.m.
for team pictures.

Bowie Kuhn
Opposed
To Option

NEW YORK (UPI)—Base-
ball commissioner Bowie Kuhn
said today he was opposed to
an option clause in player con-
tracts because he wanted the
Sport to be free of any danger
of losing its integrity.

Kuhn made the comment
under cross examination dur-
ing the Curt Flood trial in fed-
eral district court. Flood’s
anti-trust suit is seeking to
overturn baseball's reserve
clause which binds a player to
one team until he is traded.

THE VETERAN player was
traded by the St. Louis Cardi-
nals to the Philadelphia Phil-
lies but refused to report to the
Phils and subsequently filed
the suit contesting the reserve
system.

Pro football players present-
ly have a clause in their con-
tracts which permit them to
play out other options and sign
with another team.
