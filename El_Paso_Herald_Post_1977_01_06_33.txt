Out-of-town

press gets
red carpet

A LONG BLACK limousine pulled
up at International Airport whenever
an out-of-town press boxer arrived to
cover the 42nd annual Sun Bowl foot-
ball game.

Bill Johnson, sports information
director of the Sun Bowl, and Rich
McCarty and his press host commit-
tee transported the writers to their
downtown hotel in the limousine
which looked like it was fresh from a
funeral.

Despite such a bizarre welcome, the
46 visiting writers found that it was
the season to be jolly. They were so
well taken care of here that all of
them left with words of praise for the
treatment they received during their
stay in El Paso.

Jack Hairston of the Gainesville,
Fla., Sun, the home newspaper of
University of Florida, said: ‘I've cov-
ered every bow! there is with the ex-
ception of the Rose, and I've never
seen anything as well organized as the
group you have here.”

The writers were transported from
their hotel to various Sun Bowl
events, including the game, and back
in an English-styled double decker
bus borrowed from Radio Station
XERO here.

They had their hotel reservations
arranged. They headquarterd at a
hospitality room equipped with a bar
and were not only surfeited with liq-
uid refreshments but food as well.

They were taken on tours to Juarez
and in E] Paso.

A host committeeman even fetched
some chewing tobacco for a writer
who went to the game without any.
The committee also arranged to ship
one writer a case of his favorite Te-
cate beer from Juarez.

Squeeze job

BILL JOHNSON had to find seats in
the press box for 25 Texas and New
Mexico writers and 21 from Florida in
addition to spaces for the El Paso
press. He squeezed everyone in some-
how with a minimum amount of dis-
comfort.

The Juarez Race Track and Sun-
land Park were choice visiting spots
for the Texas writers since this state
doesn’t have parimutuel betting.

Florida writers, used to seeing the
horses and dogs run, opted for other
tours than the race tracks.

The major complaint of the visiting
