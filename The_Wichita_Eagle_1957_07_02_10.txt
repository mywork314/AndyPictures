Boots and Bingles
Wichita’s hopes for an all-star
game here July 16 may rest with
the result of the Battle of the
Twin Cities. St. Paul and Min-
neapolis clash four times, while
Wichita is in Indianapolis for
four. If the Twin City rivals
split, all sheuld be well; if St.
Paul sweeps the series, Wichita
might lose the plum for Andy
Cohen left town plenty disgusted
with losing three toughies, one
by a rain storm and seeks RE-
VENGE. Advices from Minne-
apolis say that there is even
more than the usual St. Paul-
Minneapolis neighboring feud
this year, for Red Davis at a
spring baseball rally, openly took
a crack at Max Macon, the
Saints manager. Red said Max
bawled him out at Columbus in
1938 for losing Macon a game,
when Red made an error at
third, Max being the pitcher.
Macon claimed he didn’t recall
it. But ’s remarks set off
another family feud, or at least
it did in the papers, which en-
courages the fans to keep it
up. — Lynn Stone, general man-
ager of the local Braves, doesn’t
fear the game being a flop here,
although admitting .Wichita
must buck tremendous turnouts
of past games, such as 19,000 at
Denver last year and two or
three up around 12,000. “I have
a hunch we will do all right, if
we stay up there which I think
we can, despite the crippled con-
dition of the club,” Lynn said
last night. — Wichita will recall
Jimmy Conzelman, who as coach
at Washington U. of St. Louis.
and later a pro football coach
at Chicago, often visited Wich-
ita, making several football
talks here. He was a whale of a
speaker. Well, the white-haired
Jimmy is now an actor. He plays
the role of the Yankee manager
in “Damn Yankee,” the color-
ful musical which was a hit on
Broadway for two years and is
now on the summer starlight
theater circuit. Conzelman got |
some very fine notices for his
stage work at St. Louis last week.
Wichitans visiting there
shouldn’t miss the show.
