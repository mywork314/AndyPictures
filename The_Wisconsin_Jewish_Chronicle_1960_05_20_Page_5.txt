Greatest Quarterback

Sid Luckman was a football star
with the Chicago Bears for twelve
years and was Columbia Univer-
sity’s top player for three years
before that. He quit a long time
ago, but he still makes newspaper
columns, especially those written
by men who remember his ex-
ploits and insist he was the best
of a brilliant lot of quarterbacks
in his time.

In a recent column on Luckman,
Jimmy Cannon wrote that “men
are still flattered to be in his com-
pany.” And that he was “the great-
est quarterback I've ever seen.”
He also informed his readers that
Luckman is now in business, serv-
ing as vice-president of Cellu-
craft and that he has an 18-year-
old son who is himself quite a
good football player—but not as
his dad, who was one of the very
best. tomes

A Word on Tennis

Angela Buxton Silk, who made
her mark as Angela Buxton in
British tennis, recently wrote an
article for the Jewish Chronicle
of London in which she bewailed
the decline of Jewish interest in
tennis, Her conclusion is that there
aren't enough junior players work-
ing at it to make good. It is a
pleasant sport, she remarks, and
so it should be more popular than
it is. But it isn’t and that’s that.
The same is true in the United
States. And Angela, like Ameri-
cans, points to the Australians, to
whom the game is of major im-
portance. True enough. But what
can you do about it?

Andy Cohen

Now that Andy Cohen, the old
Jewish ballplayer of the 1920's, is
a coach with the Philadelphia
Phillies, the columnists are catch-
ing up with him and interviewing
him about his past. The stories are
pleasant enough and _ they recall
how exciting it was for the Jew-
ish sports fans to root for one of
their own in the Era of Wonderful
Nonsense. Cohen says today that
“T’'ve had a long wait to get back,
more than 30 years. It’s worth
waiting for, too, because there's
nothhing in life quite like the big
leagues.”

On the Jewish angle, he said,
“They said I was the Jewish ball-
player John McGraw always
wanted. But McGraw was primar-
ily interested in good ballplayers,
although I know he felt that a
Jewish one wouldn't do any harm
at the gate.”
