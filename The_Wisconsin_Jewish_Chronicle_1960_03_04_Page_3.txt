Jewish Parents, Not Bias Keep Jews

Out of Baseball, Says Andy Cohen

MINNEAPOLIS —(JTA)— Ra-
cial and religious prejudice is
“alien to the baseball diamond”
and it is parental objections and
not bias which are responsible for
the relative lack of Jewish players
in professional baseball.

That’s the opinion of Andy Co-
hen, who for nine seasons was a
favorite of Minneapolis ball fans
as the talented second baseman of
the Minneapolis Millers. Now liv-
ing in El Paso, Tex., Cohen re-
visited Minneapolis as the guest of
honor at the 13th annual Baseball
Boosters Dinner.

Cohen, who played with the
New York Giants, is returning to
the major leagues this season as
an infield coach with the Philadel-
phia Phillies. He said that he had
never encountered as much as a
wisecrack about his Jewishness in
30 years of association with the
sport.

“An athlete’s ability, not his
race, color or religion, is the factor
that counts in baseball,” he said.
“A racing fast ball, not racism,
wins ballgames.”

Stamp Honoring Herzl

Introduced in House

WASHINGTON —(JTA)— Rep.
Seymour Halpern, New York Re-
publican, introduced a bill in the
House to create a U. S. postage
stamp in the “champions of lib-
erty” series, commemorating the
100th anniversary of the birth of
Theodor Herzl.

The issue would be concurrent
with the celebration of Dr. Herzl’s
centennial year. Rep. Halpern said
the name of the Zionist pioneer
would occupy a place side by side
with such liberators as Bolivar,
Kossuth, and San Martin.

Two Italian Youths Jailed

for Painting Swastikas

ROME — (JTA) — Two young
men found guilty of stealing and
hanging from a tree a statue rep-
resenting a Jewish warrior and
painting swastikas were sentenced
to 40-day jail terms. A third boy
was found guilty but released be-
cause he was a minor.
