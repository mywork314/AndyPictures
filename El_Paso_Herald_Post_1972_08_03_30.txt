Parseghian, Adamian

ARA PARSEGHIAN, the Notre Dame football coach, may or
may not have heard of Paul Adamian. If Armenians or people of
Armenian extraction stick together, chances are Ara knows of
the player who was in the offensive backfield for the U.T. El
Paso Miners the last two years. They are both of that ethnic

group.

If Parseghian had heard of Adamian, the only reason the
Miner back didn’t play for Notre Dame likely is that he’s not

quite fast enough

Adamian has been a halfback and fullback for the Miners in
the Iast two seasons. He’s been a regular part of the time. He

has led the team in rushing yardage in a number of games

He's a workhorse type. Practices hard, give it 100 per cent.

He's real coachable.

But for all that, Adamian doesn’t quite have the speed that
is needed in Western Athletic Conference competition from a

rumning back.

Going to Defense

ONE OF THE MOST interesting experiments this fall on the
UTEP team will be transferring of Adamian to a defensive
back. He’s going to start out as the No. | safety for the Miners.

The UTEP secondary has been wiped out by time. The eligi-

bility of the group of last season has expired

Naturally, this is going to be one of the focal points of the
UTEP coaches in their fall practice. Defensively, it may be the
most important department on a team. Somebody in the line
may make a mistake without it being costly. But when a defen-
sive back is inept or errs. it generally costs his team six points.

Speed is one of the bie assets of defensive backs.
Dobbs apparently thinks Adamian has enough speed to play
safety and do some safety blitzing. He'll certainly be a hard hit-

ter.

Adamian is from Birmingham, Mich., where the National
PGA Tournament is being held today. The future safetyman of PB

the Miners may be watching the event.
