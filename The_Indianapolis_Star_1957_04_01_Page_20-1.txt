Late Start But Ponce
Shooting For Majors

By MAX GREENWALD
Star Sports Writer

Hollywood, Fla, — In spring
training each year, it is custom-
ary for just about every player
in camp to impress his inten-
tion of having a good season
and elevating himself into the
big leagues.

There is one member of the
1957 Indianapolis squad with
this ambition and it may seem
a bit unusual in his case. The
player is Tony Ponce and he
is on the books as 35 years old,
| somewhat late in baseball age
| to be shooting for the majors.

Of course, Tony may have
learned something another vis-
itor to Florida, Ponce de Leon,
was trying to find out a few
hundred years ago.

Ponce, a pitcher, is unusual
though as it apnears his right
arm can whip the ball across
indefinitely.

HE HAS AN effortless pitch-
ing motion and has been ef-
fective both as a starter and
reliever. About 50 percent of
the time he uses a knuckle ball.
He has good control and knows
how to pitch. Ponce, also, can
be helpful at bat.

When asked how many
pitches he needed to warm up,
Tony replied, “two or three.”

This is Ponce’s 19th season
in pro baseball. He, also, has
participated in winter play 18
times. He didn’t compete this
past winter, being employed in-
stead in a fiber box plant in
Los Angeles. The Mexican-born

hurler said he was doing this

type of work to “keep strong.”
Ponce said he doesn’t believe
he is too old io fill his big am-
bition—reaching the majors.

Tony even had a pitching
pattern set out for 1957, which
has not had Manager Andy
Cohen's approval yet, to serve
as a stepping stone to his big
league chance,

Ponce’s idea is that he can
pitch three times a week,
perhaps three innings each,
that is if he isn’t needed as a
Starter.

Tony had his stretch for
durability in 1952 after posting
a 15-20 record at Ventura in
the California League. He was
called up by San Francisco and
finished the season pitching
and winning eight games in 16
days.

Included was a Sunday dou-
bleheader against Los Angeles.
Ponce’s earned run average for
the eight games was 1.03.

IN 1951 at Phoenix, he
pitched 152 innings and gave
only 42 hases on balls.

Tony was asked how many

clubs he had hurled for. He
scratched his head and replied,
“Twelve or 13 I guess.”
