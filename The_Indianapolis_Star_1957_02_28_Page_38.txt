Field Busy
With Sales
Of Tickets

Four more players have
signed their Indianapolis
Indians baseball contracts,
it was announced last night
by Ray Johnston, the
Tribe's general manager.

One of them, Pitcher Arnie
Atkins, visited Victory Field
in person to affix his signature.
Atkins, who was with San
Diexo last year, stopped off at
the ball park en route south.
His home’ is in Muscatine,
Iowa.

The right-handed hurler who
netted a 13-6 pitching record
last season was to leave early
today for Tampa. He wants to
work out at the Chicago White
Sox camp before the Indians
start training March 12 at
Hollywood, Fla.

Others whose contracts
were received yesterday in-

cluded Pitchers Tony Pence
and Willis Sperry and
Catcher Sam Hairston.

Twenty-one of the 35 players
expected to work out with the
Indians at Hollywood have re-
turned their signed contracts.
Spring training doesn’t get
into full swing until March 17,
Pitchers and catchers report
five days earlier,

COACH JOHNNY Hutchings
plans to leave Indianapolis
March 8 for camp, Manager
Andy Cohen and President
Donie Bush already are in the
south.

In addition to getting ready
for the training details, Victory
Field continues busy with tic-
ket sales. It is expected the
$40,000 mark might be attained
this week. Tickets are on sale
on the season plans—scaled
from $16.25 to $85—and also
for opening night. Every seat
is reserved for the curtain-rais-
er but prices remain the same
as they are for any other game
—box seats, $1.70; first eight
rows of the grandstand, $1.40;
all other grandstand seats,
$1.10.

They may be purchased over
the counter at Victory Field or
by mail.
