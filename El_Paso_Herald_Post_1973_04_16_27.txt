Too Many Fast Balls—

Andy Sees
Pitching
Mix Need

Andy Cohen believes his
UT.-El Paso pitchers learned
a lot in dropping a three-game
series to Arizona State Univer-
sity last weekend.

And he thinks that his
moundsmen will be ready for
the Sun Devils’ trip to El Paso
next week.

Arizona State swept the
Series 5-2, 25-3 and 9-4 to raise
its season record to 36-5 and
fits Western Athletic Confer-
ence record to 60. U.T. El
Paso is now 17-14 and 2-4.

“WE SHOULD have given
them a better battle,”
remarked Cohen, ‘‘but instead
of our pitchers trying to mix
up the pitches a bit, we tried
to blow the ball past them.
Bob Knox was the only pitcher
who really did well. He pitched
6 1-3 innings of smart baseball.

“Mike Kruta pitched well for
four or five innings in our 9-4
loss, but he started to hang a
curve and they (ASU) really
belted it.”

Cohen was disappointed with
his team’s fielding im the
series. He was pleased with
the hitting of Mark Miller and
Glen Villarreal.

HE REPORTED that first
baseman Barry Alburtis was
still hurting from reinjuring
his shoulder while diving for a
ground ball Friday night.
Cohen did not know when
Alburtis would return to the
Jineup
Pitcher Ray Greggerson,
who puactured his pivot foot
on a water sprinkler, did pitch
in Saturday night’s game and
“looked pretty good’, accord-
ing to Cohen.

“The thing I was most
impressed with ASU was their
pitching staff. Ed Bain, Jim
Otton and Jim Umbarger all
pitched well for Arizona
State.”

The Miners will host the Uni-
versity of Arizona in a three
game WAC series Friday and
Saturday at Dudley Field.
